Slide 4: Overview: Why Patients Lose the Golden Hour
Subtitle: 1,68,491 road-accident deaths in India in 2022 (MoRTH, Road Accidents in India 2022)

- No acceptance first: families arrive unannounced; refusals happen at the gate
- Panic & language: hard to type forms in panic; many prefer to speak Hindi
- People living alone: cannot call hospitals, arrange transport and inform family at once
- Lost information: allergies and ABHA / Ayushman details don’t reach the ER doctor
- Affected: patients & families, rural citizens & ASHA workers, hospital ER teams, the health department

Slide 5: Solution: One Flow from Emergency to Confirmed Bed
Subtitle: Every step of our flowchart is implemented and live
- 1 Emergency: speak, type, add a photo or scan a health card
- 2 AI understands: type, severity, red flags and needed capabilities
- 3 Match: specialist on duty, equipment working, bed available
- 4 Verify: freshness on every figure, who updated it and when
- 5 Accepted: a named officer accepts and assigns a receiving bay
- 6 Transport: ambulance or own vehicle, with a live map
- 7 Arrival: confirmed destination, family informed through a tracking link
- Promises: Hindi · English · Hinglish; only capable hospitals; accepted before travel; 60-second auto-fallback to the next hospital
Visual idea: 7 coloured icon circles connected by a dashed line (horizontal process flow), 4 promise cards below.

Slide 6: Technical Approach: Architecture and AI
Subtitle: AI understands; a deterministic, tested engine decides
- Apps: citizen PWA (voice, photo, OCR, offline), family tracking link, hospital dashboard
- Server: API layer, Hospital ID + Staff ID + OTP login with roles, case store with live events, audit and consent
- Engines (triage, matching, prediction) run on the server and on the phone, so triage works offline
- Generative AI: Claude turns messy text into requirements and writes the SBAR pre-arrival note
- Voice Bots: speak in Hindi / English; key steps read aloud
- Computer Vision: photo of injury → visible findings
- OCR: Ayushman / ABHA card read on the phone, shared only with consent
- Predictive Analytics: traffic ETA, bed-on-arrival probability, ER wait, 8-hour forecast
- Conversational AI: one follow-up question at a time; Mobile App: installable, bilingual, offline PWA
- Stack: Node.js 22, Express 5, PWA, Leaflet + OpenStreetMap (no API keys), Tesseract.js, Web Speech, Claude SDK
- Safety rule: severity = max(AI, rules), so AI can never lower urgency
Visual idea: Architecture diagram (apps → server → AI / engines / integrations) plus 7 technology icons.

Slide 7: Implementation: From Prototype to Statewide
Subtitle: Built today, ready to pilot in Bhopal
- Prototype (done): both portals live, AI triage, matching, acceptance, live maps, 51 tests
- Pilot · Bhopal (~3 months): onboard hospitals, nodal officers, ER desk training, clinical review, SMS OTP
- Integrate (~3–6 months): Health Facility Registry, hospital HMIS, 108 control room, ABDM, encrypted database
- Scale · MP (~6–12 months): district-by-district rollout, state command view, IVR for feature phones
- Risk: hospitals don’t update data → freshness on every figure, stale data ranked lower, one-click confirm
- Risk: wrong AI reading → AI only raises urgency; deterministic matching; 108 always visible
- Risk: no hospital answers → 60-second timeout, next suitable hospital asked
- Risk: weak network → offline PWA runs triage and matching on the phone
- Feasibility: low cost (open-source, free maps), fits existing hospital roles, cloud-ready
Visual idea: 4-phase horizontal timeline with icons, integration icons, and a risk / mitigation table.

Slide 8: Innovation: What Makes Medreach Different
Subtitle: Not “where is the nearest hospital?” but “which hospital can treat this now, and has it said yes?”
- Understands the emergency by voice in Hindi: maps No, bed portals No, helplines Operator, Medreach Yes
- Matches capability (specialist, equipment): maps No, bed portals Beds only, helplines Manual, Medreach Yes
- Shows freshness and who updated data: only Medreach, on every figure
- Hospital accepts before travel, with a receiving bay: only Medreach
- Automatic fallback to the next hospital in 60 s: only Medreach
- AI pre-arrival SBAR note for the ER doctor: only Medreach
- “I’m alone” mode for people living alone: only Medreach
- Three truths kept separate: reported capacity ≠ referral accepted ≠ confirmed destination
- Live proof: when a hospital sets its ER to “Diverting”, it disappears from every citizen’s list in under a second
